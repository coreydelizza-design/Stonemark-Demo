# What to take to counsel

A Colorado business or startup attorney is the first and most important hire; that single
engagement removes the biggest risks to the model. Take this list; every item is a
question, not a draft to rubber-stamp.

## Ask first, before any outreach or contractor

1. **Entity and conversion.** Does the LLC stay an LLC, or convert to a Delaware C-corp
   if outside money is ever raised? Confirm the registration status that is currently
   held as `PENDING` in the platform footer.
2. **Founder IP assignment.** Get the entity to own everything created before formation:
   the platform, the method, the marks, the datasets, this repo.
3. **Confirmatory assignments.** Anyone who touched the earlier IDA or Eldario codebase.
4. **Contractor stack.** Independent Contractor Agreement, present-tense IP assignment,
   NDA, non-circumvention and non-solicitation. Signed before access, not after.
5. **Worker classification, by state.** The highest-stakes question in the whole model:
   contractors delivering assurance engagements for an assurance company fail prong B of
   the ABC test in California, New Jersey, Massachusetts and others. Ask which states are
   workable, and whether engaging through the contractor's own LLC changes the answer.

## Ask before the first paid client

6. **Customer MSA and engagement letter**, with the attestation liability shield: a
   point-in-time, process-based professional assessment as of a date, not a guarantee of
   completeness or resilience. Liability capped at fees paid, consequential damages
   disclaimed, no third-party reliance without consent.
7. **Retained-IP carve-out.** The client owns the report on their estate; Stonemark keeps
   the method, platform, templates, scoring and know-how. Watch for enterprise "all work
   product is ours" clauses.
8. **LOA framework** for carrier outreach: client-signed and scoped.
9. **E&O insurance** bound before the first paid engagement, via a broker.
10. **Data handling** for sensitive infrastructure data: mutual NDA, security
    commitments, retention, return and deletion.

## Ask before the demo is linked publicly

11. **`DEMO_TERMS.md`**: is the no-reliance and no-warranty wording adequate for a
    publicly reachable demo, and does the privacy sentence match what the host actually
    logs?
12. **Fictional names.** Is the "any resemblance is unintended" position sufficient, given
    that the demo shows invented carriers failing diversity? Confirm that no real carrier
    is ever named in a finding.
13. **"Letter of Assessment"** as a product name: protectable, or too descriptive?

## Ask later

14. **Trademark**: the intent-to-use filing in Classes 9 and 42 under STONEMARK alone.
15. **Patent**: skip, or one cheap provisional on the most technical mechanism? The plan's
    own view is that the method reads like automating a careful human auditor, which is
    what examiners reject, and that a patent would publish what secrecy currently
    protects.
16. **Copyright registration** and the trade-secret redaction in the deposit.

## Record the answers

Write what counsel says back into the file it affects, with the date, and remove the
`[PENDING]` token in `PLACEHOLDERS.md`. An answer that lives only in an email is an
answer that will be re-litigated in a year.
