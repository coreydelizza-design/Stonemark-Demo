# Copyright registration checklist

Copyright exists automatically in the code the moment it is written. Registration is not
what creates the right; it is what lets you sue in the United States and claim statutory
damages instead of proving actual loss. At roughly $45–65 per work, it is the cheapest
enforcement tool available.

## What to register, and when

| Work | When | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Platform application source (`eldario-platform`) | Once the current build sequence settles | High — it is the asset |
| The method document | Once stable | High — but register the **document**, and keep the method itself a trade secret |
| Report templates: executive register, board paper | Once the wording is final | Medium |
| This demo application | After the build pack completes | Medium |
| The mason's mark artwork | With the platform, or separately | Medium |

## How, in outline

1. Register at eCO, the US Copyright Office's electronic system, as a computer program.
2. Author: Stonemark Infrastructure Assurance LLC, as work made for hire, which requires
   the assignments in `CHAIN_OF_TITLE.md` to be in place first.
3. Deposit: for a computer program, the usual deposit is the first and last 25 pages of
   source code. **Trade-secret redaction is allowed** — portions may be blocked out. Use
   it: do not deposit the scoring model in readable form.
4. Record the registration number and date in `IP_INVENTORY.md`.
5. Register a new version when a materially new release ships; each registration covers
   what existed at the time.

## Order of operations

Assignments first, registration second. Registering a work the entity does not yet
clearly own records the wrong owner and creates a defect that is more expensive to fix
than to avoid.

## Not legal advice

Registration is usually a do-it-yourself task, but the deposit-redaction choice and the
work-made-for-hire claim are worth one question to counsel. See
`COUNSEL_REVIEW_CHECKLIST.md`.
